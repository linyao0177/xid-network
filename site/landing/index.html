Follow one reading

xid in two minutes

From one meter reading to a network nobody owns.

01

Somewhere, a meter just took a reading.

42.7 kilowatt-hours. Someone will be billed for it, a grid will plan around it, a report will cite it. Everything downstream depends on that number being true.

02

Today, it disappears into someone else's cloud.

One company runs the servers, keeps the history and sends the bill. If it goes down, your devices go dark. If the number changes on the way, nobody can tell.

03

On xid, the device signs it the moment it is made.

Every device carries its own key. Each reading leaves sealed with it, so anyone, anywhere, can check that it came from that device and was not touched on the way.

04

Then independent nodes check it. Many of them.

Anyone can run a node by putting up a stake. Nodes carry the data, store it, verify it and turn it into bills. None of them has to be trusted, because none of them works alone.

05

When they agree, they get paid.

A smart contract pays for each piece of work only when enough nodes signed the same result. Honest work is the only work that earns.

06

When one lies, it pays instead.

Other nodes recheck its work. A false result costs the liar part of its stake, and the honest result still goes through. In our tests, a lying node was slashed in under a minute.

07

Nobody owns it. Anyone can join.

Devices from any maker. Nodes on any machine, anywhere. One open network where the data proves itself and keeping it honest is a way to earn.

Not a whitepaper

It already runs, on real chips and real machines.

We built the whole network and tested it end to end: firmware on a microcontroller, nodes on cloud machines, contracts on a public testnet.

ESP32

The device side runs on an ESP32-C3, a common microcontroller. It signs a reading in 21 milliseconds.

99.7%

of all work settled and paid in a 12-node test across three cloud machines, half of it within 7 seconds.

<1 min

for honest nodes to catch a lying node and slash its stake, in our end-to-end test.

First use: energy

Power that switches off when the bill goes unpaid, with no server in between.

Our first demo is pay-to-power. A meter publishes signed readings, three independent nodes bill them, and the owner's relay stays on as long as the bills are paid.

1

The meter signs every reading on the device.

2

Independent nodes compute the bill. Two of three must agree.

3

The consumer pays the bill straight to the owner's wallet.

4

A bill goes unpaid, and the relay switches the power off.
